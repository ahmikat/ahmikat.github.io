BabyLM Workshop @ EMNLP 2026 · Five single-seed DeBERTa-v2 runs

01 The question

When every word counts, which learning signal should lead?

With very little training text, RTD gives the model a dense signal: it checks whether nearly every token belongs in its context. MLM asks it to recover selected missing words. We tested whether RTD should stay strong throughout training or gradually make more room for MLM.

↳ Our matched comparison keeps both tasks active, changing only the weight of RTD over time.

02 Data & setup

One small corpus. One shared budget.

All five systems use the official BabyLM 2026 Strict-Small English corpus, the same backbone dimensions, and the same maximum training exposure.

10Mwords in the training corpus
10×maximum passes over the corpus
5single-seed DeBERTa-v2 runs

Shared tokenizer: 16K SentencePiece unigram. Discriminator: 12 layers, 384 hidden units. No external teacher or evaluation labels were used for training.

Keep the dense signal.
Ease its influence.

05 Findings

What we learned.

I

Decay beats fixed RTD in the matched pair.

bgb-decay is +0.34 Overall and +0.46 NLP average ahead of Baby Got BERT, with gains on six of seven NLP categories.

II

Other curricula specialize.

LAD improves BLiMP Supplement; the Lilliput runs lift selected knowledge, transfer, or reading tasks. Those gains come with losses elsewhere.

III

The claim has a clear limit.

Only the fixed-versus-decay pair isolates one change. The other models alter multiple factors, and all five are single-seed runs.

In one sentence

Under this small-data budget, gradually reducing RTD’s weight gave the best observed balance across tasks.
